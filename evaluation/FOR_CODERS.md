# For coders: how this works

We use a language model to find sentences in Dutch crisis reporting (1912–1992) that compare the present with an earlier past, such as "net als bij de watersnood van 1953" or "nog nooit eerder". To know how far we can trust it, we need people to judge the same sentences. You get **one list of about 420 sentences**. For each one, read the highlighted TARGET sentence (the grey text around it is only context). Decide whether it compares the present with a past from before the crisis (yes/no). If yes, also say whether it stresses likeness or difference, and what kind of past it refers to. The **Codebook** above has the rules. Tick *unsure* instead of agonising. Plan on about 4–5 hours, in as many sittings as you like. Every answer is saved as soon as you click, so you can stop and come back at any time, and you are finished when the bar is full. There is no second round. The sentences were chosen from what the model decided: some where it found a comparison, many where it did not. They are mixed so that nobody can tell which is which, and you never see the model's answer. Many sentences contain no comparison at all; that is expected. About two thirds of your sentences are also coded, independently, by one of the other two coders. That tells us how much people agree, and that agreement is the highest standard we can hold the model to. Where two coders disagree, or someone ticks *unsure*, the adjudicator decides afterwards, so you never have to recode or meet. We then compare the final answers with the model's. That shows how many of its comparisons are real and how many real ones it misses. **Please don't discuss sentences with each other until all three of you have finished.**
